# EU baseline: vehicle hire for a consumer

What holds in every EU country, read on the consolidated EU texts on 2026-11-26. Sources and the
sentences to re-check are in `controls.json`. "CRD" is Directive 2011/83/EU on consumer rights,
consolidated version of 27 September 2026. There is no EU regulation or directive on car rental as
such; the rules below come from general consumer, contract-terms, data and signature law.

A country can be stricter where an act says so (unfair terms, language). The country layer wins over
this file wherever it says something different. The CRD protects consumers; for a business renter
this baseline was not checked, and the same three documents can still be used.

## 1. Is the contract covered, and is there a withdrawal right

| # | Rule | Effect on the document | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Car rental is a service inside the CRD. Consumers get its protection, with the exception of the right of withdrawal. | Apply the rows below | CRD recital 27 |
| 2 | No right of withdrawal for car rental services when the contract provides for a specific date or period of performance. Member States may not provide one, and the trader may still grant one by contract. | Block J of the agreement states there is none; the firm's own cancellation terms are printed as its own | CRD art. 16(l), art. 4, art. 3(6) |

When a contract does not fix a date or period, this baseline did not settle whether a withdrawal
right exists: say it was not checked and ask the firm to confirm that every booking fixes a date or
period.

## 2. What the documents must do

| # | Rule | Effect on the document | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Before the consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract, the trader gives: main characteristics; identity and addresses; total price including taxes and any other costs; payment and performance arrangements and complaint handling; duration and termination; out-of-court complaint mechanism. This information is an integral part of the contract. For on-premises contracts a shorter set applies if the information is not already apparent from the context. | Blocks A, F, M and N of the agreement. Printing the full set for every rental is the safe default | CRD art. 6(1)(a)-(d), (e), (g), (o), (t), art. 6(5), art. 5(1) |
| 4 | Where no withdrawal right is provided under art. 16, the trader gives the information that the consumer will not benefit from one. | Block J | CRD art. 6(1)(k) |
| 5 | If the trader did not disclose additional charges, the consumer does not bear them. | A charge on the return record that the agreement did not announce is flagged to the firm | CRD art. 6(6) |

| 9 | A pre-formulated contract is not individually negotiated. A term causing a significant imbalance to the consumer's detriment, contrary to good faith, is unfair and does not bind the consumer. ## 4. What EU law does not set

| # | Point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | The content of the rental terms (deposit amount, damage, excess, fuel) is general contract law. | CRD art. 3(5) |
| 15 | EU law does not require the hirer to record driving licence data. Licences issued by Member States are mutually recognised. Directive (EU) 2025/2205 repeals Directive 2006/126/EC from 26 November 2029; until then the old directive stands. Minimum age, years of licence held, young-driver surcharges and whether a copy may be made are national. | Directive 2006/126/EC art. 2(1); Directive (EU) 2025/2205 arts. 29-30 |
| 16 | Compulsory insurance, what the contract must disclose about excess and waivers, minimum age, deposit and card-hold limits, fines and tolls, a signed handover protocol: not checked in the baseline; they are lookup questions. | not checked |
| 17 | Fees for means of payment may not exceed the trader's cost (CRD art. 19). The article exists; its detail was not read. | not checked in detail |

## 5. Personal data

| # | Rule | Effect on the documents | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | Processing needed to comply with a legal obligation rests on Union or Member State law. The data that obligation requires is the lawful basis for that data only. | Print which data are collected and why; mark data the firm collects for its own purposes separately | GDPR art. 6(1)(c), 6(3) |
| 19 | Data must be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary, and kept no longer than necessary. | Capture data fields, not a picture of the ID or licence, by default | GDPR art. 5(1)(c), (e) |
| 20 | Member States may adopt more specific provisions for processing under a legal obligation and may set conditions for national identification numbers. | The exact data list, retention and ID number rules are national | GDPR art. 6(2), art. 87 |
| 21 | The European Data Protection Board says, about authenticating an access request, that copies of ID cards requested by many organisations including car rentals should generally not be considered an appropriate way of authentication. | An indirect pointer only. It concerns access requests, not hire | EDPB Guidelines 01/2022 v2.1, para. 75 |

National data protection authority positions on copying ID documents were not read on the
authorities' own pages. Do not state any as fact.

## 6. Signature

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | An electronic signature cannot be denied legal effect or admissibility as evidence only because it is electronic. | Regulation (EU) 910/2014 art. 25(1) |
| 23 | A qualified electronic signature has the legal effect of a handwritten one. | Regulation (EU) 910/2014 art. 25(2) |
| 24 | Whether a document needs handwriting, a qualified signature or a notary is national law. | Regulation (EU) 910/2014 art. 2(3) |

Formify signatures are covered by row 22. Where a country asks for the form in row 23 or for
handwriting, say so and do not send that document for e-signature.
