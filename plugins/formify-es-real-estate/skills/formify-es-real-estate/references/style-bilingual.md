# Spanish contract drafting style: bilingual contracts, anglicisms to avoid, plain language, sources

## 8. Bilingual contracts

- Spanish is the operative version whenever the document goes before notaries, registries, courts or tax offices; foreign buyers get a two-column or facing-page text (Spanish left, or Spanish first, never interleaved with mixed headings).
- Prevalence clause: "El presente contrato se firma en español y en inglés. En caso de discrepancia entre ambas versiones, prevalecerá la versión en castellano." Or: "La versión inglesa se facilita únicamente a efectos informativos." "Castellano" is used when contrasting with co-official languages, "español" with foreign ones; both are correct.
- Co-official languages: contracts in Catalan/Valencian, Basque or Galician are fully valid. Notarial deeds are drafted in the official language the parties agree, and in both if they disagree (Reglamento Notarial art. 149). In Catalonia consumers may demand contracts and standard forms in the official language of their choice (Ley 22/2010, art. 128-1, as read by STC 88/2017), so agencies there keep Catalan and Spanish versions of their hojas de encargo. Rule for an AI: Spanish master; co-official version on request; add the prevalence clause whenever two languages are supplied.

Sources: Reglamento Notarial art. 149; Ley 22/2010 (Código de consumo de Cataluña) art. 128-1 and STC 88/2017; Law Insider "discrepancia en el idioma"; internationalcontracts.net (cláusula de idioma).

## 9. Anglicisms and translation tells to avoid

| Avoid (tell) | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "Términos y Condiciones" as contract label | "condiciones", "cláusulas" (traduccionjuridica.es: the calque is "un sinsentido legal", tolerated only for websites) |
| "en base a" | "con base en", "sobre la base de", "de acuerdo con", "conforme a" (RAE: admissible but less recommended) |
| "a nivel de" (non-literal) | "en el ámbito de", "en cuanto a", or delete |
| "en orden a" | "para", "con el fin de", "a fin de" |
| "realizar" as universal verb | "efectuar/abonar el pago", "entregar", "llevar a cabo"; also "implementar" → "aplicar" |
| "a la atención de" in notices clause | "dirigidas a", "a la dirección de" |
| "por y entre" (by and between) | "entre" |
| "el presente Acuerdo" for agreement | "el presente contrato" (Acuerdo only for non-contractual agreements/MoU) |
| "Considerando que" recitals (WHEREAS) | "EXPONEN / I. Que ..." |
| "Consideración" (consideration) | "contraprestación", "precio", "causa" |
| "sujeto a" everywhere | "con sujeción a", "conforme a", "sin perjuicio de", "salvo" |
| "bajo el presente contrato", "bajo la ley" | "en virtud de", "al amparo de", "conforme a", "previsto en" |
| "incluyendo, sin limitación" | "incluidos, a título enunciativo y no limitativo" |
| "las Partes acuerdan por la presente" (hereby) | "las partes acuerdan" |
| "de aquí en adelante referido como" | "en adelante," / "en lo sucesivo," |
| "deberá" for every "shall" | future indicative or "se obliga a" |
| "eventual" = final | "eventual" means possible in Spanish; use "definitivo/final" |
| "tiempo es esencial" | "el plazo tiene carácter esencial" |
| "Sección", "Artículo" for clauses | "cláusula", "estipulación" |
| "Anexo A", "Exhibit" | "Anexo I" |
| "corredor", "broker", "closing", "deposit" | "agente inmobiliario / API", "otorgamiento de la escritura", "arras / señal" |
| "parking", "apartamento" (Spain) | "aparcamiento / plaza de garaje", "piso / vivienda" |
| "€10,000.00", "10,000 €", "EUR 10.000" | "10.000,00 €", "diez mil euros (10.000 €)" |
| "Septiembre 5, 2026", "05/09/26" in the date line | "a 5 de septiembre de 2026" |
| em dashes as pauses; Oxford-style bulleting | commas, colon, parentheses |
| Initial capitals on every defined noun ("el Precio", "la Fecha de Cierre") and a long definitions clause | few inline definitions; parties in all caps or lower case; nouns lower case |
| "firmado por" + title block "Nombre: / Cargo: / Fecha:" | "Fdo.:" + name, party label above |

## 10. Plain-language recommendations endorsed by Spanish authorities (no loss of legal force)

From the Comisión de Modernización del Lenguaje Jurídico (Ministerio de Justicia, 2011) and the Libro de estilo de la Justicia (RAE/CGPJ, 2017), all compatible with full legal effect:
1. Short sentences, no "párrafos excesivamente largos" or chained subordinates; one paragraph per topic; long enumerations on separate lines.
2. Active voice ("El Congreso aprobó", not "Se aprobó por el Congreso"); the "se" impersonal stays acceptable.
3. No specifying gerund or gerund of posteriority; no future subjunctive ("tuviere" → "tuviera"); no ambiguous "el mismo / el cual / cuyo" (prefer "que" and noun repetition). See §5.
4. Replace or gloss Latinisms and non-technical archaisms; keep the technical legal term ("arras", "cuerpo cierto") and gloss it for consumers.
5. Not "servicios a prestar" (Gallicism) but "servicios que deben prestarse"; repeat the preposition in enumerations ("en la venta, en el arrendamiento y en la cesión").
6. Comma rules: none between subject and verb; parentheticals between commas. Capitals only where grammar requires.
7. Consistent verb tense in the recitals; discourse markers ("en primer lugar", "además", "no obstante", "por último").
8. Cite laws in full on first mention, abbreviate afterwards; keep long citations out of the sentence.

Legal-design practice (Abogacía Española on ISO 24495-1; Prodigioso Volcán) adds: descriptive clause titles, a summary box of key terms (price, dates, penalties), numbered lists of obligations, one obligation per sentence. None of this weakens legal force: contracts are interpreted by literal wording and intent (arts. 1281-1289 CC), and ambiguity is construed against the drafter (art. 1288 CC), so clarity protects the drafter.
